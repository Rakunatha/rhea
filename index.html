03Figure · Age & Perceived Urgency
Age group vs. agreement that the issue "requires urgent attention"
Result

Opinion is most divided among younger married adults

Reading the bars
The 19–35 age bracket shows the widest spread of opinion — roughly 19% strongly agree the issue is urgent, yet a slightly larger share (20%) disagree. Respondents aged 55+ lean toward disagreement more than agreement.
Discussion
This split suggests urgency isn't simply a function of age in a linear way — younger married respondents are simultaneously the most concerned and the most skeptical, possibly reflecting differing personal exposure to the issue within the same cohort. Older respondents' tilt toward disagreement may reflect generational norms that under-recognize domestic violence as a pressing concern, reinforcing the paper's call for age-tailored awareness messaging.
04Objectives of the Study

What this research set out to answer

01
Quantify the prevalence of physical, psychological, and sexual abuse within marriage among the sampled population.
02
Examine the relationship between sociodemographic characteristics and the likelihood of experiencing marital domestic violence.
03
Identify which risk factors most strongly predict the likelihood of abuse.
04
Formulate evidence-based policy recommendations to strengthen legal protections and support mechanisms for victims.

Gender vs. self-rated overall awareness (1–10 scale)
Result

Female respondents self-report the highest awareness

Reading the bars
Female respondents cluster at a high awareness rating (9/10, ~25%), ahead of male respondents who center on a mid-level rating (8/10, ~17%). Transgender respondents show the widest spread, with meaningful shares at both very low and very high ratings.
Discussion
The gender gap in self-rated awareness aligns with the study's broader finding that women are disproportionately affected by marital violence and, correspondingly, more attuned to it. The wide spread among transgender respondents points to uneven access to information within that group, and echoes the paper's call for more inclusive, gender-diverse outreach rather than a one-size-fits-all campaign.
06Methodology

Design, sampling & analysis

Research Design
A descriptive design, chosen to map out how common the problem is and where it concentrates across different groups.
Sample
215 respondents, gathered via convenience sampling across urban and peri-urban communities.
Instrument
Structured, face-to-face questionnaire using a five-point Likert scale, supplemented by peer-reviewed literature and government data.
Statistical Tools
SPSS v21 — chi-square tests for association, one-way ANOVA for group differences, and Pearson correlation for linear relationships.

07Figure · Education & Framework Confidence
Educational qualification vs. belief that existing frameworks are adequate
Result

Higher education brings confidence — and more critical scrutiny

Reading the bars
Uncertainty dominates among respondents educated below 10th grade (~21% "not sure"). Postgraduates are the most confident that frameworks are adequate (~31% "yes") — the highest of any group — while PhD holders split almost evenly between "yes" and "no."
Discussion
Confidence in existing protections rises with education, but the most highly educated respondents are also the most divided — suggesting deeper engagement can surface more critical views rather than simple reassurance. This mirrors the paper's finding that lower educational attainment corresponds with reduced legal literacy, reinforcing the case for education-targeted legal awareness campaigns.

09Figure · Area & Trust in Current Measures
Area of residence vs. trust in current protective measures
Result

Urban trust is higher — and more polarized

Reading the bars
Urban respondents report both the highest strong agreement (~25%) and the highest strong disagreement (~20%) in trusting current measures. Rural respondents report lower engagement overall, with smaller shares at either extreme.
Discussion
Greater visibility of legal and support infrastructure in cities appears to produce sharper, more polarized opinions — people have enough exposure to form a strong view, positive or negative. Rural respondents' comparatively muted response is consistent with the paper's finding of lower legal-recourse awareness outside urban centers, pointing to a service-accessibility gap rather than indifference.

11Figure · Occupation & Self-Rated Awareness
Occupation vs. self-rated overall awareness
Result

Retirees report the highest awareness; workers cluster at "neutral"

Reading the bars
Retired respondents post the highest "strongly agree" share (~22%) for self-rated awareness. Employed and unemployed respondents cluster heavily around a neutral rating, while students show the most varied response, split between agreement and disagreement.
Discussion
Retirees may have more time and life experience to engage with the topic, while day-to-day work or job-seeking pressures could leave employed and unemployed respondents with less bandwidth to form a strong opinion either way. The variability among students suggests awareness at this life stage depends heavily on individual exposure — such as coursework, media, or personal networks — rather than a shared occupational experience.
12Conclusion & Recommendations

Toward stronger protection & support

This study found pronounced urban–rural and educational disparities in the experience and awareness of marital domestic violence across 215 respondents. The findings support recognising marital rape as a criminal offense and expanding community-based support to reduce the burden of abuse. Limitations include a convenience sample, self-report bias, and a cross-sectional design — future work should adopt longitudinal, mixed-methods approaches.

  • Mandatory domestic violence training for public service employees
  • Secure, anonymous digital reporting platforms linked to helplines
  • Culturally tailored, gender-inclusive awareness campaigns
  • Longitudinal research tracking intervention outcomes
  • Interdisciplinary legal, health & education collaboration
